# LA0099: Aspect Provided to the Wrong Kind of Declaration

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Error |
| **Reported by** | PostSharp |
| **Message** | `Cannot apply the aspect "<Aspect>" to "<Target>": this aspect must be applied to <DeclarationKind>.` |

## Cause

Each kind of aspect works on one kind of declaration: a <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.MethodLevelAspect>, such as an <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.OnMethodBoundaryAspect>, on a method; a <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.TypeLevelAspect> on a type; a <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.LocationInterceptionAspect> on a field or a property; and so on. When you apply an aspect as a custom attribute, multicasting already skips the declarations that the aspect does not support. An aspect that an <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.IAspectProvider> provides is not filtered this way: its target is the declaration passed to the <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.AspectInstance> constructor. When this declaration is of a kind that the aspect does not support, PostSharp reports LA0099 on it and skips the aspect.

`<DeclarationKind>` is the kind of declaration that the aspect supports, such as `a method` or `a type`.

## Resolution

In the <xref:PostSharp.Aspects.IAspectProvider.ProvideAspects*> method, select the declarations that the provided aspect supports: for a method-level aspect, the methods of the target type, from <xref:System.Type.GetMethods*>; for a location aspect, its fields or properties. Otherwise, provide another kind of aspect, one that supports the target.
